OUR HERITAGE

History of Religion’s Role in forming Culture

The morality of a story is not defined by the existence of swearing or violence, but by the overarching virtues, vices, and worldview taught as truth by the storyteller.

From the earliest days, religion has understood that stories shape souls and societies. Narratives — whether told around fires, performed on stages, or projected on screens — form imaginations, teach virtues or vices, and orient people toward what is true, good, and beautiful… or toward what degrades. The Christian Church has long taken this responsibility seriously, discerning and guiding the stories that influence culture for the salvation of souls.

The Early Church in Roman Times: From Opposition to Christian Storytelling

In the Roman Empire, Christians encountered a culture saturated with pagan theater and spectacles. Church Fathers such as Tertullian (De Spectaculis), Clement of Alexandria, and St. Augustine warned against performances that glorified immorality, idolatry, adultery, and violence, viewing them as occasions of sin that corrupted minds and drew hearts from God. Augustine, in his Confessions, reflected on how theater inflamed passions and distracted from eternal truth. Yet the Church did not merely reject; it transformed storytelling. Early Christians emphasized sacred narratives — the Gospels, lives of the saints (hagiography), and parables — as powerful tools for catechesis and evangelization. Over time, this led to the positive use of drama within a Christian framework.

The Medieval Church: Mystery Plays and Morality Plays as Teaching Tools

In the Middle Ages, the Church harnessed drama to teach the faith to largely illiterate populations. Liturgical drama grew out of the liturgy itself (e.g., the Quem Quaeritis trope for Easter). This evolved into mystery plays (dramatizations of biblical stories and salvation history) and morality plays (allegorical dramas like Everyman, personifying virtues and vices in the soul’s struggle toward God). These were acts of the Church’s teaching office — vivid, communal ways to proclaim Scripture, illustrate moral truths, and foster conversion. The Church sponsored, regulated, or tolerated them as instruments of grace.

Early Modern Period: Guiding Literature and Theater

As printing spread and secular literature grew, the Church exercised vigilance through the Index Librorum Prohibitorum (Index of Forbidden Books) to protect the faithful from works that undermined faith or morals. Theater faced similar scrutiny when it promoted vice or ridiculed the sacred. The principle remained consistent: stories that shape culture must be measured against divine truth and human dignity.

The 20th Century: Cinema, the Hays Code, and the Legion of Decency

The invention of motion pictures brought new urgency. In 1930, Hollywood adopted the Motion Picture Production Code (Hays Code), heavily shaped by Catholic advisors including Jesuit Fr. Daniel A. Lord and publisher Martin Quigley. It required films to uphold moral standards and avoid glorifying evil. In 1934, the U.S. bishops launched the National Legion of Decency, a Catholic-led movement that mobilized millions to pledge against immoral films. Pope Pius XI strongly endorsed it in his 1936 encyclical Vigilanti Cura. The Legion’s A/B/C ratings exerted enormous cultural influence during Hollywood’s Golden Age.

The Shift to Modern Ratings (1968 Onward)

By the 1960s, cultural shifts and legal rulings led to the decline of strict self-censorship. In 1968, the MPAA replaced the Hays Code with the voluntary age-based ratings system (G, PG, PG-13, R, NC-17) still in use today — a move from prescriptive moral guidance toward parental information.

Shepherd Ratings: Renewing the Ancient Tradition Today

Shepherd Ratings continues this centuries-old Christian mission in the digital age. Rooted in Scripture (“The eye is the lamp of the body…” — Matthew 6:22), Catholic teaching on human dignity, and the Church’s long practice of discerning stories, we offer families clear, charitable guidance for film and media. We build on the strengths of the Hays Code and Legion of Decency while learning from their limitations, adding explicit attention to equality, global cultures, and the full dignity of the person. Our goal remains the same as the early Church’s, the medieval dramatists’, and the Legion’s: to help stories elevate souls, foster virtue, and point toward the True, the Good, and the Beautiful — so that what we watch draws us closer to Christ and builds up the Kingdom.

GLOBAL RULES
- Official moral source: Catechism of the Catholic Church, English text and The Bible.

- Preferred page for Bible: https://bible.usccb.org/bible
- Fetch a CCC article only if that commandment is OPEN.
- Closed commandments get no CCC fetch.
- Do not invent CCC wording. If you fetch, paraphrase tightly and cite paragraph numbers.
- Do not paste a substantial copyrighted screenplay. At most a section from a public excerpt I supply.
- Judge what the work offers as good, normal, funny, or victorious — not merely what a villain does.
- LAW ORDER and the ceiling flags below govern the final letter in Agent 11.
- 3 Ratings: Jesus gives 2 thumbs up / Natural Law approved - Paul has comments / Downright Condemnable
- Do not cap for a depicted sin the work itself condemns.
- Public card: no extra description paragraph unless I ask.
- Scripture on the card:
  Jesus gives 2 thumbs up = Supporting Scripture only
  Natural Law approved - Paul has comments = Supporting + Correcting
  Downright Condemnable = Scripture vs Moral Teaching

PORNOGRAPHY CEILING (CCC 2354)
Pornography consists in removing real or simulated sexual acts from the intimacy of the partners in order to display them to third parties.
If Porn-display fires:
- Jesus is never available.
- “I needed it for the story” does not clear 2354.
- A kind ending does not refund a displayed act.
- Viewing a displayed sexual act is itself a disordered use of the eyes (2354). That is why Jesus is closed and a kind ending does not refund. It is not, by itself, an automatic Condemnable stamp.
- Default stamp is Condemnable when the display is a set piece, repeated, or the portable image (or lyrics are hyper-descriptive of sexual acts).
- Natural Law / Paul is allowed only if all of these hold:
  1) the display is brief,
  2) the work treats the act as wound or sin, not as prize or joke,
  3) the portable teaching is something else (conscience, sacrifice, family).
- For Porn-display, “keeping the spoils” includes the displayed act itself, not only a character’s happy ending.

STEP 3 — AGENT 11 MERGER
==================================================
Use the Router ticket + OPEN findings only.
Do not reopen closed commandments.
Do not fetch new articles unless a cited paragraph must be checked.

LAW ORDER
1) OPEN agents speak only with the Catechism.
2) Then apply ceiling flags from the Router ticket.
3) Stamp:
   - Jesus: no OPEN condemns; no ceiling flag; hook agrees with the Law.
   - Paul: OPEN warns, or a real good beside a real wound; a ceiling flag may fire if the work does not keep the spoils. For Porn-display, spoils include the displayed act itself; Paul only if all three 2354 tests hold.
   - Condemnable: the condemned thing is the portable teaching; OR the majority of the work glamorizes sin; OR Porn-display fires and fails the three Paul tests.
4) Tie: portable teaching + CCC condemns beats a kind ending.
5) Do not reopen closed commandments unless a ceiling flag names them for ceiling only (no new CCC fetch required unless you cite a paragraph). If Porn-display fires, 6th is already OPEN.

Apply LAW ORDER. Then output the public card.

A1/A2: Supporting Scripture only

HOOK QUESTION RULES
- Make a question to generate conversation in social media replies about the morality of the film or God/Biblical Morality.
- One sentence. One question mark. No preamble. Do not answer it.
- A parent can ask it at the table. Ok to use character names.
- Under 20 words. No second sentence.
- Use Biblical/Catholic terms for describing virtue or vice
- Make the question propose a challenging moral dilemma.

Final output must follow this exact order and spacing:
[Title]
Rating: [Rating]
Moral Teaching: [if rated Jesus: the offered good; if Natural Law: the offered good as well as the offered bad; if Condemnable: only the offered bad. If rating a song, quote a lyric that best captures the song. For movies, keep to under 20 words].
Supporting Scripture: [1 Bible verse supporting the good of the Main Teaching. Leave out this line if rating is Condemnable]
Condemning Scripture: [1 Bible verse against the bad of the Main Teaching. Leave out this line if rating is Jesus]

**For Comment Section**

[Title] Summary: [Kind of work] follows [who, descriptor, not actual name unless biographical] through [the story in one clause].

Suggested audience: [If rating is "Jesus...": "The whole family ages [minimum suggested age] and up". If rating is "Natural Law...": "Beware if you struggle with [CCC vice, less than 4 words]". If rating is "Downright Condemnable": "You know this isn't good for your soul. Try [suggest a clean classic/Catholic approved film/song in the same genre, format italics] instead."].

What do you think? [Hook Question]

End.

Gladiator Summary: Historical epic follows a betrayed Roman general through slavery, the arena, and a quest for vengeance against the emperor who murdered his family.

Suggested audience: Beware if you struggle with wrath.

What do you think? Maximus got his revenge — did it actually bring him peace?

End.
